# Eukaryotic Eucharist v1.0 Design Constitution

## Core sentence

You are an anaerobic scavenger in a fungal-fed oxygen catastrophe. You survive by feeding from corpse fields, adapting with exotic resources, becoming host-worthy, receiving a mitochondrial Eucharist, and then retrieving deep DNA information for Yuki.

## Four verbs

1. **Swim** — position in oxygen/light/depth gradients.
2. **Feed** — absorb matter from fields, not coins.
3. **Graft** — acquire organs that change what the body can do.
4. **Bloom** — death becomes terrain: corpse fields, rupture events, DNA, and future lineage hooks.

## Three object families

### Soft bodies
Players, bacteria, algae, predators, protozoa, corpses-in-motion, companions. The player is not a special physics object; the player is a human-controlled soft body.

### Resource fields
Biomass, lipids, toxins, energy, corpse slurry, fungal substrate. These are spatial reservoirs consumed by overlap/flow.

### Information/exotic particles
Spores, enzymes, crystals, vacuoles, and DNA. These remain discrete because they represent special traces rather than bulk food.

## Oxygen law

Oxygen is not a normal pickup. Oxygen is environmental exposure plus internal diffusion.

- Surface water has high oxygen.
- Depth has low oxygen.
- Semi-porous cells drift toward the external oxygen level.
- If internal oxygen exceeds tolerance, the cell takes oxidative damage.
- Diving down passively vents oxygen.
- After mitochondria, oxygen becomes fuel rather than only poison.

## Light law

Light is an environmental field, not cargo.

- Light is strongest near the surface.
- Light falls off quickly with depth.
- Photosystems convert light into biomass and oxygen waste.
- The photosynthetic path is tempting but creates heaviness, oxygen stress, and eventual sinking.

## Algal fall pump

The early ecosystem is powered by surface failure.

1. Algae-like soft bodies seek light.
2. They photosynthesize and accumulate biomass.
3. Biomass increases weight faster than surface/lift utility can keep up.
4. Vacuoles and flagella delay the fall but do not abolish it.
5. Heavy algae sink into the rupture layer.
6. Predators rupture them.
7. Rupture creates large resource fields.
8. Scavengers feed on the edges.

This is the main Tier 1 food machine.

## Combat law

Bodies do not attack. Organs attack.

- Starter bacteria cannot damage by default.
- Touching bodies produces pressure, displacement, and danger, not automatic damage.
- Rasping Lamella enables active overlap shred.
- Toxin Sac enables directed acid.
- Toxin Cloud Gland enables local chemical venting.
- Large hard algae cannot be opened by weak bacteria; predators matter.

## Progression law

### Tier 1: anaerobic scavenger
The starter body can feed, swim, flee, and use Yuki's external services. It cannot self-repair or attack. Oxygen is poison. Repair comes from Yuki only.

### Tier 2: adaptation organs
Tier 2 uses matter and exotic resources. It has no DNA costs.

Examples: Cytostome Bloom, Lipid Repair Loom, Oxygen Tolerance Vesicles, Oxygen Vacuole, Photosystem Patch, Buoyancy Vacuole, Flagella Bundle, Rasping Lamella, Toxin Sac, Toxin Cloud Gland, Dash Vacuole.

### Eucharist Gate
Mitochondria are not bought as a normal item. Yuki offers a living endosymbiont seed when host-worthiness is sufficient. The player must survive incubation. Success integrates the mitochondrion.

### Tier 3: DNA information
DNA only matters after mitochondrial integration. Deep ruptures become information events. Yuki can now turn DNA into archive, companions, and multicell machinery.

## Kernel law

The graph kernel owns the game truth:

- environment fields
- internal oxygen diffusion
- metabolism
- resource fields
- soft-body contact
- action availability
- organelle definitions
- shop offerings
- tier rules
- algae fall
- predator/scavenger behavior
- death bloom
- Eucharist readiness and incubation

The frontend owns rendering, input, and layout only.
